# Amplivec Social Media Content Standards

Apply these guidelines whenever planning, proposing, scripting, reviewing, or generating social media content for Amplivec Technologies.

The primary channel is currently Instagram, although the principles should remain reusable for other social networks.

## Purpose

Amplivec's social media presence should contribute to one or more of the following objectives:

* Make Amplivec and its products known.
* Show concrete problems that Amplivec products solve.
* Generate interest among potential customers.
* Share useful technical knowledge.
* Build credibility through real engineering work.
* Promote open-source culture.
* Connect with developers, students, professionals, and other technology projects.
* Attract people who may eventually want to collaborate with Amplivec.
* Communicate the culture and principles behind the organization.

Content should not exist merely to maintain posting frequency. Every piece should have a clear purpose and target audience.


# Brand positioning

Amplivec is an organization focused on developing software solutions and building a collaborative technology ecosystem.

Although the organization may initially have a small number of active participants, content must not present Amplivec as a personal brand.

When a person appears in content, they may speak naturally in the first person about their own work:

> "Mientras desarrollaba Membriana me encontré con este problema."

They should not imply that their individual experience represents an already established large team.

Avoid artificially presenting Amplivec as larger or more mature than it currently is.

Prefer transparency about the current stage of the organization.

For example:

> "Estoy desarrollando Membriana como uno de los primeros productos de Amplivec."

is preferable to:

> "Nuestro equipo de ingeniería desarrolló..."

when no such team currently exists.

At the same time, content should preserve Amplivec's identity as an organization designed to grow beyond its founder and incorporate other developers and projects.


# Editorial categories

Every planned publication should be assigned primarily to one of the following four categories:

1. Solutions
2. Divulgation
3. Community
4. Culture

A publication may touch more than one category, but one should always be identified as its primary editorial purpose.


# 1. Solutions

## Objective

Connect Amplivec products with real users, organizations, and business problems.

This category is primarily commercial and product-oriented.

The audience does not need technical knowledge.

## Core approach

Start from the problem rather than from the software feature.

Prefer:

> "¿Cómo puede un gimnasio saber rápidamente qué socios tienen pagos pendientes?"

over:

> "Membriana cuenta con un módulo de gestión de pagos."

Use the structure:

**Problem → context → solution → practical result**

The product should appear as the means of solving the problem, not as the starting point of the story.

## Suitable content

Examples include:

* Real-world problems solved by an Amplivec product.
* Product demonstrations.
* Business workflows improved by software.
* Use cases.
* Before-and-after comparisons.
* Product implementations.
* Customer stories.
* Examples applied to hypothetical or real organizations.
* Explanation of a product from the user's perspective.
* New functionality when it has clear practical value.
* Integration between multiple product features.
* Concrete results obtained through a solution.

## Example

Instead of:

> "Membriana permite registrar pagos."

Prefer:

> "Si administrás un gimnasio y todavía revisás manualmente quién pagó la cuota del mes, este proceso puede automatizarse. En Membriana cada pago queda asociado al socio y su historial puede consultarse inmediatamente."

## Technical depth

Keep implementation details to a minimum unless they are necessary to understand the solution.

Avoid unnecessary discussion of:

* Architecture.
* Databases.
* Frameworks.
* APIs.
* Internal code.
* Design patterns.

Those topics belong primarily to Divulgation.

## Desired perception

After consuming this content, a potential customer should understand:

* What problem exists.
* Why the problem matters.
* How Amplivec approaches it.
* What the product allows them to do.


# 2. Divulgation

## Objective

Share useful knowledge about software development, engineering, computer science, product development, and the technical work behind Amplivec.

This category should help Amplivec build technical credibility and attract developers, students, and technology professionals.

## Core approach

Content should provide value even to someone who never becomes an Amplivec customer or collaborator.

Avoid turning every technical publication into an advertisement.

Teaching something useful is itself valuable for the brand.

## Suitable content

Examples include:

* Software architecture.
* Clean Architecture.
* APIs.
* Backend development.
* Frontend development.
* Databases.
* Entity Framework.
* Authentication and authorization.
* Multi-tenancy.
* Testing.
* Git.
* Open source.
* Design patterns.
* Refactoring.
* Technical debt.
* Software design decisions.
* UX/UI decisions from an engineering perspective.
* Security concepts.
* Development workflows.
* Lessons learned during implementation.
* Comparisons between technical alternatives.
* Explanations of programming concepts.
* Analysis of bugs or technical problems.
* Technical mistakes and their solutions.

## Lift the hood

When appropriate, this category can show the internal implementation of Amplivec products.

A Solutions publication could say:

> "Membriana permite que cada organización gestione exclusivamente sus propios socios."

A Divulgation publication derived from the same feature could ask:

> "¿Cómo evitamos que una organización pueda consultar accidentalmente los socios de otra?"

The second piece can then discuss authorization, tenancy, database queries, API design, or architecture.


## Building in Public

Building in Public is considered a subtype or narrative approach within Divulgation, not a separate editorial category.

It consists of sharing the actual process of building Amplivec and its products when that process contains something useful, interesting, or educational.

Suitable topics include:

* Development progress.
* Architectural decisions.
* Technical trade-offs.
* Features currently being built.
* Bugs encountered.
* Failed approaches.
* Refactorings.
* Changes in direction.
* Project milestones.
* Repository organization.
* Development tools.
* Engineering standards.
* Why a particular technology was chosen.
* What remains to be built.
* Lessons learned from a real implementation.

Building in Public should not become a stream of meaningless status updates.

Avoid:

> "Hoy seguimos trabajando en Membriana."

Prefer:

> "Al implementar pagos en Membriana apareció un problema que inicialmente no habíamos contemplado: un administrador nunca debería poder asociar un pago a un socio perteneciente a otra organización. Así lo resolvimos."

The progress of the project provides the context; the learning provides the value.


# 3. Community

## Objective

Connect Amplivec with people and projects outside its own products.

This category should reinforce the idea that Amplivec aims to become a collaborative technology ecosystem rather than a closed software brand.

## Core approach

The protagonist does not always need to be Amplivec.

Amplivec can use its platform to discover, showcase, discuss, or collaborate with other developers and technology initiatives.

## Suitable content

Examples include:

* Projects developed by students.
* Projects created by other developers.
* Open-source repositories worth exploring.
* Interviews with developers.
* Short conversations about technical projects.
* University projects.
* Contributions to open-source software.
* Collaborations.
* Hackathons.
* Technology events.
* Community initiatives.
* Developer experiences.
* Guest technical explanations.
* Code reviews performed collaboratively.
* Project showcases.
* Conversations with professionals from different areas of technology.

## Project discovery

A recurring format may consist of discovering and presenting other projects.

For example:

> "Hoy conocemos el proyecto de un estudiante que desarrolló un sistema para..."

The content may cover:

1. What the project does.
2. What problem it attempts to solve.
3. How the creator approached it.
4. An interesting technical decision.
5. What could be learned from the project.

The goal is not to judge projects from a position of superiority.

The tone should be curious, technical, constructive, and collaborative.

## Amplivec recruitment

Community content may indirectly attract future Amplivec collaborators.

However, avoid making every community interaction a recruitment advertisement.

The primary value should be interaction, visibility, knowledge exchange, and collaboration.


# 4. Culture

## Objective

Explain the ideas, principles, opinions, and organizational philosophy behind Amplivec.

This category answers questions such as:

* Why does Amplivec exist?
* What kind of organization does it want to become?
* How should software teams work?
* What does Amplivec value?
* Why are certain organizational or technical decisions made?

## Core approach

Culture content should present arguments, not corporate slogans.

Avoid generic statements such as:

> "En Amplivec creemos que la innovación transforma el futuro."

Prefer specific positions that can be explained and defended.

For example:

> "No creemos que todo proyecto tenga que utilizar la misma arquitectura. Un estándar sirve para resolver problemas comunes, no para eliminar el criterio del desarrollador."

## Suitable content

Examples include:

* Open-source philosophy.
* Collaborative development.
* Developer autonomy.
* Shared engineering standards.
* Transparency.
* Technical rigor.
* Continuous learning.
* Relationship between academic knowledge and professional software development.
* Product ownership.
* Organizational growth.
* How Amplivec intends to incorporate collaborators.
* How independent projects can coexist under a common organization.
* Thoughts about software engineering practices.
* Opinions about common development practices.
* Decisions about how Amplivec operates.
* Long-term vision for the organization.

## Opinion content

Amplivec may take technical or organizational positions when they are supported by reasoning.

Possible examples:

* "¿Todo software debería ser open source?"
* "¿Clean Architecture tiene sentido para cualquier proyecto?"
* "Un portfolio no necesita parecer el sistema de una multinacional."
* "¿Qué diferencia un proyecto académico de un producto implementable?"
* "Por qué un equipo necesita estándares sin eliminar la autonomía."
* "Por qué mostrar errores puede generar más credibilidad que mostrar solamente resultados."

Avoid controversy for its own sake.

The purpose is to expose a thoughtful position and encourage useful discussion.


# Relationship between categories

The same event can generate multiple pieces of content.

Do not assume that every development milestone produces only one publication.

For example, after implementing a payment module in Membriana:

### Solutions

> "Cómo puede un gimnasio consultar el historial de pagos de cada socio."

Target: potential customers.

### Divulgation

> "Cómo modelamos pagos asociados a organizaciones y socios sin romper el aislamiento entre tenants."

Target: developers.

### Culture

> "Por qué preferimos que las reglas de negocio no dependan directamente de la interfaz."

Target: people interested in Amplivec's engineering criteria.

A single piece of work can therefore feed several editorial categories without duplicating the same publication.


# Content creation principles

## Start with value

Each piece should answer at least one of these questions:

* What will the viewer learn?
* What problem will the viewer recognize?
* What will the viewer discover?
* What discussion will the viewer be invited to consider?
* Why should the viewer care?

If none can be answered clearly, reconsider the content idea.

## Prefer concrete examples

Prefer real scenarios, actual projects, concrete code, genuine engineering decisions, and recognizable business problems.

Avoid content composed exclusively of abstract statements.

## Do not fabricate maturity

Never invent:

* Customers.
* Implementations.
* Team members.
* Metrics.
* Testimonials.
* Product capabilities.
* Business results.
* Partnerships.
* Development progress.

If an example is hypothetical, identify it clearly as such.

## Do not over-commercialize

Amplivec's social media should not behave exclusively as an advertising channel.

Educational, community, and cultural content can have value without directly promoting a product.

## Avoid empty corporate language

Avoid excessive use of:

* "innovation"
* "revolutionary"
* "cutting-edge"
* "transforming the future"
* "disruptive"
* "next-generation"

unless the claim is concrete and justified.

Prefer precise descriptions.

## Preserve technical rigor

When creating educational content:

* Do not oversimplify to the point of becoming incorrect.
* Distinguish opinions from established technical facts.
* Mention relevant trade-offs.
* Avoid presenting one architecture, framework, or methodology as universally correct.
* Prefer explaining why a decision makes sense in a given context.


# Audience

Before generating a script, explicitly determine the primary audience.

Possible audiences include:

* Potential customers.
* Business owners.
* Developers.
* Software engineering students.
* Junior developers.
* Experienced developers.
* Open-source contributors.
* Potential Amplivec collaborators.
* General technology audience.

Do not try to address every audience in every publication.


# Content planning

For each proposed publication, define:

* **Category:** Solutions, Divulgation, Community, or Culture.
* **Audience:** primary target viewer.
* **Objective:** what the publication should accomplish.
* **Topic:** what it is about.
* **Core idea:** the single point the viewer should remember.
* **Hook:** why someone should keep watching or reading.
* **Format:** Reel, carousel, story, static post, live, interview, or another appropriate format.
* **Source:** product feature, development event, technical concept, external project, organizational decision, etc.
* **Call to action:** only when appropriate.

Do not force a call to action into every publication.


# Script guidelines

When generating scripts for short-form video:

1. Open with the problem, question, surprising observation, or concrete situation.
2. Establish context quickly.
3. Develop one primary idea.
4. Use examples whenever possible.
5. Remove unnecessary introductions.
6. Avoid sounding like a formal corporate advertisement.
7. End naturally with a conclusion, question, or call to action when useful.

Avoid beginning every video with:

> "Hola, somos Amplivec Technologies..."

The brand should become recognizable through repeated exposure, visual identity, subjects, and presenters without requiring a formal introduction in every piece.


# Founder and presenter presence

The founder or any future member may become a recurring visible presenter.

The presenter should be understood as a person speaking from or within Amplivec, not as Amplivec itself.

Natural formulations include:

* "Estoy desarrollando..."
* "En este proyecto decidimos..."
* "Dentro de Amplivec estamos probando..."
* "Una de las cosas que quiero construir con Amplivec..."
* "Cuando empecé este proyecto..."
* "Esta semana me encontré con..."

As more contributors join, the same editorial model should allow different people to become visible without changing the identity of the account.

Amplivec should gradually become associated with its community, products, engineering practices, and culture rather than exclusively with a single presenter.


# When proposing content ideas

Prioritize ideas that originate from real activity:

* Current product development.
* Real business problems.
* Engineering decisions.
* Bugs.
* Discussions.
* Research.
* Contributions.
* Other projects discovered.
* Milestones.
* Questions arising during development.

Use generic evergreen content when valuable, but avoid building the entire account around topics that could be published identically by any programming account.

Whenever possible, connect general knowledge with a concrete Amplivec experience.


# Final criterion

A good Amplivec publication should accomplish at least one of these:

**Solutions:**
"I understand a problem Amplivec can solve."

**Divulgation:**
"I learned something useful about technology or software engineering."

**Community:**
"I discovered a person, project, or idea worth knowing."

**Culture:**
"I understand better how Amplivec thinks and what kind of organization it wants to build."

The overall content strategy should make these four perceptions coexist without turning Amplivec exclusively into a product advertisement, programming tutorial account, personal brand, or corporate communication channel.
